# Brand Identity

This document defines the complete brand identity of the website: its personality, atmosphere, values, and voice.

It is **not** a UI document. It is **not** a design system. `04_DESIGN_SYSTEM.md` will define colors, spacing, and components — this document defines the personality and identity behind every decision made in that document and every one after it. When a future design choice is ambiguous, this document is what resolves it.

---

# 1. Brand Purpose

This platform represents the working identity of a single creative practitioner — a game designer and worldbuilder — expressed through a personal platform that combines:

- Portfolio
- Worldbuilding
- Game Design
- Storytelling
- Development Journal

The brand's emotional purpose is to make a visitor feel they have found something *made*, not *assembled*. Every other portfolio site communicates competence through a gallery grid. This one communicates competence through coherence — the sense that one mind, working with discipline, made everything a visitor is looking at.

The brand exists to earn a specific reaction: *this person clearly knows exactly what they're doing, and there's more here than I expected.* Not excitement for its own sake — quiet respect, followed by curiosity to look further.

Coherence comes from voice, presentation, and authorship — not from every piece of work belonging to one fictional universe. KRUPNI is part of this identity, but a standalone story, an independent project, or a portfolio piece with no fictional-world relationship at all belongs to the brand just as fully. A stable, finished website can go on expressing this identity indefinitely without needing a redesign to stay coherent.

---

# 2. Brand Personality

If the brand were a person, they would be:

- **Quiet** — doesn't need to raise its voice to be taken seriously.
- **Confident** — states things plainly because it doesn't need to oversell them.
- **Curious** — genuinely interested in how things work, visible in the depth of the worldbuilding.
- **Thoughtful** — decisions read as considered, not reactive.
- **Disciplined** — consistency and follow-through over years, not bursts of enthusiasm that fade.
- **Creative** — but in service of craft and story, not novelty for its own sake.
- **Professional** — reliable, credible, takes the work (and the visitor's time) seriously.
- **Atmospheric** — carries a mood wherever it goes; nothing feels neutral or sterile.

**Never:**

- **Arrogant** — confidence is shown through the work, never claimed in the copy.
- **Loud** — no visual or verbal shouting for attention.
- **Generic** — every choice should be traceable to this specific person and this specific body of work, never to "what portfolio sites usually do."

---

# 3. Brand Values

- **Craftsmanship** — the work is made with technical care and attention to detail, at every scale from a single hardsurface prop to a full environment. Craft is demonstrated, not asserted.
- **Quality** — fewer, better things over many mediocre ones (see `02_CONTENT_ARCHITECTURE.md`, section 2).
- **Curiosity** — the drive behind worldbuilding depth (KRUPNI) and behind trying new techniques, documented honestly in the Development Journal.
- **Consistency** — the same standard, the same voice, the same visual language, applied everywhere, so trust compounds instead of resetting on every page.
- **Honesty** — devlogs document real progress, including setbacks; nothing is inflated to look further along than it is (see `02_CONTENT_ARCHITECTURE.md`, section 4).
- **Long-term thinking** — decisions are made to be maintainable and stable through the site's active-use period, not as speculative bets on a specific multi-year future (see `00_PRODUCT_VISION.md`, section 3).
- **Storytelling** — even non-narrative content (a CV, a tech breakdown) is framed with narrative intent, because that's how this brand thinks by default.
- **Innovation** — pursued in service of the work itself (a better way to model a prop, a more interesting narrative structure), never chased as a trend for its own sake.

# 4. Brand Voice

Text on this site should sound: **professional, minimal, thoughtful, immersive, calm.**

**Never:** clickbait, overly corporate, exaggerated.

**Good example (Portfolio Piece summary):**
> A hardsurface loadout built for close-range readability — every panel line and seam considered from three meters away, where it actually gets seen.

*Why it works: specific, confident, technically grounded, no adjectives doing work the facts should be doing.*

**Bad example (same subject):**
> This AMAZING prop took my skills to the next level! You won't believe the detail packed into this masterpiece — a total game-changer for my portfolio!

*Why it fails: exaggerated, clickbait rhythm, empty superlatives, sounds like marketing copy rather than a craftsperson describing their own work.*

**Good example (Devlog entry):**
> The corridor lighting pass didn't read the way it did in the reference. Pulled the fog density back by half and re-lit from three sources instead of one — closer, but still flat. Revisiting tomorrow with a different key light angle.

*Why it works: honest about the setback, specific, calm, treats the reader as someone who understands the craft.*

**Bad example (same subject):**
> Huge progress today!! The lighting is looking incredible and I'm SO happy with how this environment is turning out. Can't wait to show you guys more! 🔥

*Why it fails: overclaims progress that the content itself doesn't support, informal register inconsistent with the brand, performative enthusiasm rather than genuine process documentation.*

**Good example (World/KRUPNI summary):**
> The year is 2770. Megacorporations govern what nations once did, and the line between human and something else has been redrawn more than once.

*Why it works: immersive, economical, states the premise without over-explaining it.*

**Bad example (same subject):**
> Welcome to KRUPNI — an EPIC sci-fi universe unlike anything you've ever seen, filled with jaw-dropping twists and incredible characters you'll fall in love with!

*Why it fails: generic hype language that could describe any universe; tells the reader how to feel instead of giving them a reason to feel it.*

# 5. Emotional Experience

**First impression** — quiet intrigue. A visitor should sense atmosphere and intent before they've read a single word (see `00_PRODUCT_VISION.md`, section 6, User Journey).

**After browsing** (portfolio, projects) — confidence in the craft. A visitor should feel they've seen real technical range, presented without unnecessary noise.

**After reading stories** — immersion, a sense of having briefly stepped into a real, considered place, and mild reluctance to leave it.

**After viewing projects** — respect for follow-through — the sense of a person who finishes things and documents the process honestly, not just a person with good ideas.

**After leaving the website** — the desire to come back. Not because of a hook or a gimmick, but because the site left the impression of depth still unexplored.

---

# 6. Visual Identity Philosophy

The visual direction is: **premium, modern, minimal, atmospheric, cinematic, elegant, timeless.**

The site should look like it was designed once, with intent, by someone with taste — not assembled from whatever a component library defaults to. Every visual choice should feel inevitable in hindsight, not decorative.

**Avoid trends that age quickly.** A visual choice made because it is currently fashionable (a specific gradient style, a currently-trending UI pattern) is a liability, not an asset — it dates the site the moment the trend passes. Favor visual decisions that are timeless within *this brand's* atmosphere over decisions that are merely current. This principle governs every subsequent, more concrete design decision made in `04_DESIGN_SYSTEM.md`.

---

# 7. Color Philosophy

This document does not choose colors — `04_DESIGN_SYSTEM.md` does. This section defines what color choices must *communicate*, regardless of the specific palette selected:

- **Mystery** — enough darkness and restraint that not everything is revealed at once.
- **Depth** — layered, not flat; color should suggest distance and atmosphere, not just fill shapes.
- **Focus** — color draws attention deliberately, never randomly or decoratively.
- **Contrast** — used with intent, to create hierarchy and cinematic weight, not just for legibility's sake.
- **Warmth** — a human, handmade quality underneath the atmosphere, so the site never reads as cold or purely technical.
- **Technology** — a credible sense of precision and engineering, appropriate to hardsurface and environment work.
- **Trust** — professional enough that a recruiter or studio reads competence, not experimentation for its own sake.

Any future color system must be evaluated against this list before it is evaluated against trends or personal preference.

---

# 8. Typography Philosophy

Typography should express: **readable, professional, elegant, technical where appropriate.**

- Readability comes first — atmosphere is never an excuse for text a visitor has to fight to read (see `AI_RULES.md`, UI Consistency and Accessibility rules).
- Elegance should come from restraint and proportion, not from decorative flourish.
- A technical register (more geometric, more precise) is appropriate for functional or technical content (metadata, specs, code-adjacent content); a more expressive register is appropriate for narrative content — but both must clearly belong to the same family and the same brand.
- Never decorative for the sake of decoration. Every typographic choice must serve legibility or hierarchy — nothing is added purely because it looks interesting in isolation.

---

# 9. Imagery Philosophy

Every visual — project screenshots, environment renders, concept art, character art, maps, diagrams, photography — must feel like part of one ecosystem, regardless of which pillar it belongs to.

- **Project screenshots / environment renders** — presented with the same atmospheric, cinematic lighting sensibility described in section 6, never as flat technical captures when used for presentation (technical captures belong in process/breakdown content, not hero placement).
- **Concept art** — treated as legitimate creative work in its own right, not filler; see `02_CONTENT_ARCHITECTURE.md`, section 5.
- **Character art** — consistent tonal treatment with environment work, so a World never feels visually split between "the art" and "the lore."
- **Maps** — functional first (a visitor must be able to read them), atmospheric second — legibility is never sacrificed for mood.
- **Diagrams** — minimal, precise, and restrained; a diagram should look like it belongs on this site, not like it was pasted in from a generic technical document.
- **Photography** (if used, e.g. for About/CV context) — same restraint and mood as every other image category; never stock-photo generic.

No image category is allowed to visually contradict another. A visitor moving from a portfolio render to a worldbuilding map to a devlog screenshot should never feel like they left the site and landed on a different one.

---

# 10. Iconography

Icon philosophy: **minimal, consistent, readable.**

- Icons support navigation and meaning; they are never decorative filler.
- A single, consistent visual weight and style across every icon used on the site — no mixing of icon styles or sources.
- Avoid decorative icons — an icon that exists only to add visual interest, without a functional labeling or navigation purpose, does not belong on this site.

---

# 11. Brand Consistency

Every future feature, page, or component must be evaluated against this document before it is considered finished:

- Does it sound like this brand's voice (section 4)?
- Does it produce the intended emotional experience (section 5)?
- Does it honor the visual identity philosophy (section 6) and the color, typography, imagery, and iconography philosophies (sections 7–10)?

If a proposed feature cannot honestly answer yes to these, it must be revised before shipping — or, per `AI_RULES.md`, flagged as a conflict rather than pushed through as-is. Brand consistency is not a final polish pass; it is a gate every piece of work passes through, every time.

---

# 12. Things We Never Do

- Never become a generic portfolio.
- Never chase design trends.
- Never sacrifice usability for aesthetics.
- Never overuse animations.
- Never imitate other portfolio websites.
- Never break visual consistency.
- Never let brand voice slip into clickbait, exaggeration, or corporate language (section 4), regardless of the content type.

---

# 13. Final Brand Principles

- The work speaks; the copy never has to shout for it.
- Restraint is a feature, not a limitation.
- Consistency across pillars is what makes this a platform, not a collection of pages.
- Every visual and verbal choice must be traceable to this document.
- Atmosphere and usability are not in tension — when they conflict, usability wins.
- Nothing ships that could have come from any other portfolio site.
- This identity does not change with trends; only the craft behind it deepens over time.
