# Product Vision

This document defines **why** this website exists. It is the single source of truth for the project's purpose and direction, and every other document in `/docs` builds on top of it.

## 1. Project Summary

This website is the personal platform of Ege Demir Ünal (TETSUNARU) — a game designer and worldbuilder working across environment design, hardsurface prop modeling, and original fiction.

What it is:

- A living archive of finished and in-progress work: environments, hardsurface props, game projects, and original worlds.
- A Worldbuilding platform centered on **KRUPNI** — the website's only currently planned fictional universe, a sci-fi setting in the year 2770 ruled by megacorporations, home to DNA-modified human-animal hybrids.
- A development journal that documents the process behind the work, not just the finished result.
- A single, coherent identity that recruiters, collaborators, and readers can explore from more than one angle — as a portfolio, as fiction, as a body of design thinking.

What it is **not**:

- Not a generic 3D-artist portfolio template with a grid of renders and nothing else.
- Not a blog bolted onto a portfolio as an afterthought.
- Not a showcase of finished work only — process, iteration, and lore are first-class content, not supplementary material.
- Not a system with no completion point. It has a clear initial build, after which it can remain stable while content continues to be published.

Purpose: to give every piece of work — a prop, an environment, a game system, a page of lore — a coherent, well-presented home on one personal platform, so that visiting the site feels like encountering a considered body of work rather than browsing a resume. Content does not need to share a fictional universe to belong here; coherence comes from craft, voice, and presentation, not from forcing every piece into one connected world.

## 2. Mission

This website exists because scattered work — an ArtStation gallery here, a game jam page there, lore notes in a private document — fails to communicate what actually connects them: a single creative vision built over years, across mediums.

Problems it solves:

- Fragmentation. Without this site, the work lives in disconnected silos (ArtStation, itch.io, private documents) with no shared context.
- Flattening. A grid of renders shows *what* was made but not *why*, or how it fits into a larger body of work.
- Forgettability. A templated portfolio is interchangeable with a thousand others; this site is not.

Why people should visit:

- Recruiters and studios get a fast, credible read on real skill and range — environment design, hardsurface modeling, game design, and the discipline to sustain a long-term creative project.
- Collaborators and worldbuilding enthusiasts get access to an original universe (KRUPNI) they cannot find anywhere else.
- Anyone curious about the process gets to see how the work is actually made, not just the polished output.

## 3. Development Timeline

**Initial Completion** — Complete the professional website in approximately 2–3 weeks: all required public pages, the Admin workflow, responsive behaviour, accessibility, and deployment stability, with portfolio, worldbuilding, and game design sections populated with real work.

**Active Use** — Publish and update content when there is real work to share. The website may remain technically unchanged for approximately one year if it continues to meet the owner's needs. Content growth does not automatically require feature growth.

**Optional Future Decisions** — New technical systems are added only after a real limitation or opportunity is identified, and only with the owner's explicit approval. No specific product direction beyond the active-use period is assumed; imagined three-year, five-year, or ten-year growth scenarios are not treated as requirements.

## 4. Core Pillars

**Portfolio** — Purpose: demonstrate craft (environment design, hardsurface prop modeling, 3D work) at a professional standard. Value: the primary credibility signal for recruiters and studios. Connection: a portfolio piece may reference the worldbuilding concept, project, or game it came from where that link is real — but it stands entirely on its own when it doesn't.

**Worldbuilding** — Purpose: house the KRUPNI universe — currently the only active fictional universe on the site — in depth. Value: differentiates this site from every generic portfolio; demonstrates narrative and systems thinking, not just visual skill. Connection: characters, factions, and locations here may be drawn on by portfolio renders and game projects where genuinely relevant.

**Game Design** — Purpose: showcase shipped and in-progress games, and the design thinking behind them. Value: proves the ability to take a concept from idea to playable system, not just a static image. Connection: a game may be set inside KRUPNI, and its environments/props may double as portfolio pieces — neither is required.

**Projects** — Purpose: a working umbrella for anything in progress that doesn't yet belong cleanly to Portfolio, Worldbuilding, or Games. Value: keeps momentum visible; shows the site is alive, not archival. Connection: a project may graduate into another pillar as it matures, or remain a project — it does not need a Worldbuilding relationship.

**Stories and Writing** — Purpose: original fiction and creative writing, in readable long-form. This includes stories set inside KRUPNI as well as standalone fiction, scenario experiments, and personal writing unrelated to Worldbuilding. Value: gives the site a direct creative voice beyond reference pages and renders. Connection: a KRUPNI story may reference its characters, locations, and factions; a standalone piece requires no fictional-world metadata at all.

**Development Journal** — Purpose: document process, decisions, and iteration across all other pillars. Value: builds trust and shows growth over time; turns the site into a record, not just a result. Connection: an entry may link back to the pillar it documents, or stand as a general reflection with no specific link.

A visitor should be able to start in any pillar and discover a genuine connection to another where one exists — but no pillar, and no piece of content within a pillar, is required to connect anywhere to be considered complete.

## 5. Target Audience

**Recruiters** — Goals: quickly assess hire-worthiness. Needs: fast access to best work, clear skill categorization, contact/CV. Expectations: professional presentation, no friction, evidence over claims.

**Game Studios** — Goals: evaluate fit for a specific role or collaboration. Needs: proof of technical range (environment/hardsurface) and of design maturity (game projects, systems thinking). Expectations: production-quality work, clear software/engine competencies (UE5, Blender).

**Publishers** — Goals: assess whether a game project is fundable or fits their catalog. Needs: clear pitch material, evidence of scope discipline and follow-through. Expectations: a project page that reads like a pitch, not just a gallery.

**Players** — Goals: understand what a game is about and whether it's for them. Needs: tone, premise, and hook communicated fast. Expectations: atmosphere and clarity over jargon.

**Developers** — Goals: learn from process and technique. Needs: development journal entries with real detail, not just marketing language. Expectations: honesty about iteration and failure, not only success.

**Artists** — Goals: evaluate technique and craft. Needs: process breakdowns, software/workflow notes, high-resolution work. Expectations: technical credibility.

**Worldbuilding Enthusiasts** — Goals: get lost in an original universe. Needs: depth, internal consistency, a way to navigate lore without getting lost. Expectations: the setting feels real and considered, not sketched.

## 6. User Journey

**First 5 seconds** — The visitor should immediately understand: *this is a designer/worldbuilder, this site is not generic, something atmospheric is happening.* The hero must communicate identity and tone before a single word is read.

**First 30 seconds** — The visitor should grasp the shape of the site: what the core pillars are and where to go for what they came for. A recruiter should already see a path to the portfolio; a worldbuilding reader should already sense KRUPNI exists.

**First 2 minutes** — The visitor should have gone one level deep into at least one pillar and formed a first impression of quality and coherence — and, ideally, discovered a connection to a second pillar (a project linking to its world, a world linking to its portfolio pieces).

Emotions to evoke: intrigue, immersion, and confidence in craft — the sense of having found something considered and alive, not assembled from a template.

## 7. Brand Personality

If this website were a person, they would be: a quiet, precise world-maker — someone who builds environments and stories the way an architect builds buildings: with restraint, intention, and an eye for what's left out as much as what's put in.

Adjectives: minimal, atmospheric, deliberate, immersive, premium, disciplined, quietly confident.

Emotional atmosphere: cinematic dark, contemplative, slightly mysterious — closer to walking through a well-lit gallery at night than browsing a résumé. Never loud, never desperate to impress; the work is left to speak.

## 8. Core Principles

Every future feature must respect:

- **Immersion** — nothing should break the feeling of being inside one coherent creative platform. KRUPNI is one fictional universe within it; independent stories, projects, and portfolio work may exist outside KRUPNI without breaking this feeling — overall coherence comes from authorship, presentation, voice, and visual identity, not from every piece of content sharing one fictional world.
- **Consistency** — visual language, tone, and navigation logic must hold across every pillar.
- **Storytelling** — even functional pages (a project list, a CV) should be framed with narrative intent, not treated as pure utility.
- **Quality** — nothing ships half-finished; a smaller scope shipped well beats a large scope shipped rough.
- **Reasonable extensibility** — structural decisions should accommodate ongoing content publishing during active use, without assuming a specific future growth multiple or building for hypothetical scenarios beyond that.
- **Accessibility** — atmosphere must never come at the cost of usability for real visitors on real devices.
- **Performance** — visual richness must never make the site feel slow or heavy.

## 9. Success Metrics

Success is measured by:

- **Returning visitors** — people coming back, not just landing once.
- **Recruiter engagement** — time spent on portfolio/CV pages, contact conversions.
- **Portfolio quality** — measured qualitatively: does the work presented meet or exceed the standard of the studios being targeted.
- **Story engagement** — time spent inside worldbuilding/story content, depth of navigation into lore.
- **Project discoverability** — visitors successfully finding and understanding in-progress work without external explanation.

Success is not measured by raw traffic alone — a small, engaged audience that understands the vision is worth more than a large, indifferent one.

## 10. Things We Will Never Do

- Never become a generic portfolio.
- Never feel like a template.
- Never prioritize visual effects over readability.
- Never invent a connection between worldbuilding and projects (or any other content) merely to complete a template — relationships must be real, and standalone content is valid.
- Never sacrifice performance for animations.
- Never publish placeholder or filler content just to appear "complete."
- Never let a new feature contradict the documents in `/docs` without stopping to flag the conflict first.
